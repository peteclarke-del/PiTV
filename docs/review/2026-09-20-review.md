# Scheduler review, 20 September 2026

Pete asked for a review of the scheduler against its rules and against real 1980s schedules for
the four main UK channels, with pitv_content included so that the two applications line up, and
for the rules to be brought into as few files as is sensible. This page is the summary and the
tracker. The evidence is in two companion pages:

- [Where each rule is decided](2026-09-20-scheduler-rules.md): every rule with the function that
  decides it, what is duplicated, hardcoded or dead, and the consolidation plan.
- [The broadcaster models against period listings](2026-09-20-broadcaster-models.md): each
  channel's dayparts against what BBC One, BBC Two, ITV and Channel 4 showed, with corrected
  rows and sources.

A third input is the built week itself, audited on the development library on 20 September.

## What the built week showed

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Certificates and the watershed in the day | Held everywhere |
| Children's programmes after the cutoff | 26 cases, all in the small-hours replay, which repeats the day without asking |
| The same series back to back | None |
| One episode a week on the general channels | Held for all but 8 of 139 series, each a thin day bringing one forward |
| Game shows before the evening | 4 (Play Your Cards Right at 10:31, The Price is Right at 12:05 on Three; two on Four) |
| Sport on a weekday daytime | 3 |
| A programme off its own channel | None, apart from cartoons borrowed in children's dayparts, as configured |
| Holding cards on One to Four | None |
| Programmes starting on a five minute mark | Rarely: starts are rounded only on channels that carry adverts, by padding with adverts, so One and Two never round |
| Film share of weekday prime time | One 49%, Two 74%, Three 37%, Four 95% (was 88 to 100% on all four before the peak-hours rule) |

The daytime game shows and sport got through because the first step of relaxation stops applying
daypart weights altogether, and a weight of 0, which is documented as a bar, goes with them.

## Findings in the code

The full list is in the companion page. The ones that change behaviour:

1. The relaxation ladder has no single definition. What each step lifts is about twenty
   conditions spread through `select.py`, one in `policy.py` and the order in `build.py`, in
   three spellings. Several are surprising when written down: relaxing turns borrowing off,
   halves sport, and lifts daypart bars.
2. Bands, a band channel's free time and anchored series never apply the certificate and
   children's rules (`allowed_at`), though PLAN 4.7 says those override everything. A film band
   could play an 18 in the afternoon; an anchored 15 could sit at teatime.
3. The count of series kept for the peak hours re-implements the selector's eligibility and has
   drifted: it counts resting series, remote titles on a channel set to NAS only, and finished
   remote series. A channel then looks better supplied than it is, which is the fault the rule
   was written to cure.
4. Remote episode numbering is spread over five files, with two different "lowest unasked"
   loops. The one that renumbers open requests ignores episodes on disk and the run's length, so
   it can move a request onto a number the library already holds.
5. "Is this sport" has two notions (the scheduling class and the programme type) that an owner's
   override changes differently; a series the owner retypes as sport is shelved as sport but
   scheduled as a weekly series.
6. Placement says it uses the scheduler's decade test and does not pass the strict flag, so a
   strict channel is given unknown-year titles it will never air.
7. `weekend_kids_breakfast` cannot fire: it looks for a weekend daypart named "Breakfast" and
   there is none. `external_weight` below 1 is silently raised to 1 though the admin offers 0 to
   2. `Library.started_channels` costs a query every build and is never read.
8. The broadcast-day clock (minutes past midnight counted on) is written five times; the strict
   test three; genre comparison six ways, one of which never matches an owner's "Sci-Fi".
9. A dozen comments and PLAN paragraphs describe behaviour the code no longer has (bands giving
   time back, `content` being only a label, the Toons film weight, the NAS-only paragraph).

## The broadcaster models

The boundaries are right. The corrections are genre weights and three rows:

- BBC One: soap and quiz belong at 19:00 to 21:00, not 17:35, which was news and magazine;
  breakfast had no children's strand; variety was the core of Saturday evening and is weighted
  nowhere; Sunday morning was education.
- BBC Two: the "Open University" and Daytime rows are schools hours; the teatime film was barred
  by a length cap; Saturday nine o'clock was films; Sunday Grandstand began at 13:00.
- ITV: weekday mornings were For Schools until 1987; Saturday night split into game shows then a
  US crime hour at 20:00, then a film; Sunday 22:00 was a comedy half hour.
- Channel 4: lunchtime shut out the children's programmes it carried; soap at 18:00 is
  unsupported; "Film on Four" on Sunday is unsourced.

## What lines up with pitv_content, and what does not

pitv_content has been asked for the same inventory of its side (every rule that decides what is
fetched, accepted or ordered, which side is the authority where both hold a version, and where
its code departs from the contract). Known before its answer:

- The copy rule and the screen profiles exist on both sides (STATUS C14). PiTV is the authority;
  pitv_content should be sent the values or read them.
- The genre vocabulary is shared by copy.
- The order a cache run works in, the fetch share, turns, episode evidence, namesakes and the
  "not found yet" memory are pitv_content's alone, and are now in contract sections 3 and 6.
- PiTV's search hints for an episode ("<title> S01E02 <year> full episode", "BBC", "ITV") predate
  the agreement that a matched series is asked for by its Nth episode; pitv_content searches by
  the real episode title, so the hint is now noise at best.

## Decisions that are Pete's

1. Closedown and Ceefax. The most period thing absent from Two and Four is being off air. A band
   filled from a "Pages from Ceefax" style loop would express it without a scheduler change. It
   cuts against "always look like a working channel", so it is not being built without a word.
2. Junctions. Rounding starts to five minutes on the channels without adverts needs something to
   fill up to four minutes: idents and a "next" caption, as the BBC did. A caption is not a
   placeholder for missing content, but it is a card on screen.
3. Whether bands and anchors should obey the watershed (finding 2). The safe answer is yes, with
   the band's own card when nothing of its fill may air yet. Proceeding on yes unless told
   otherwise, since PLAN already says certificates override everything.
4. Seeded anchors for famous slots (Top of the Pops on Thursday, Blockbusters at 17:15), which is
   what would bring back fixed weekly appointments. Anchors exist for library series only today.
